      📖 Autobiography

      📋 Florida B.E.S.T. (Grade 6):
      ELA.6.R.2 Reading Informational Text (strand)
      Genre: Nonfiction

      What is an autobiography?

      An autobiography is a true story about a person’s life, written by that same person.

      “Auto” means self. “Bio” means life.

      📖 AutobiographyWritten by the person it is about. Uses I, me, my.
      “I was born in a small town.”
      📚 BiographyWritten by someone else. Uses he, she, they.
      “She was born in a small town.”

      Clues:

      • 🗣️ First person: “I.”
      • 📅 Real events from the author’s life.
      • 💭 The author’s thoughts and feelings.
      • 🔙 The author looks back and shares what they learned.

      Your teacher can write here during the lesson. You can write too if you want!

      🧭 Purpose, message, and tone

      📋 Florida B.E.S.T. (Grade 6):
      ELA.6.R.2.2 Analyze the central idea(s), implied or explicit, and its development throughout a text.
      ELA.6.R.3.1 Explain how figurative language contributes to tone and meaning in text(s).

      Three big ideas

      🎯 PurposeWHY the author wrote it: to inform, persuade, or entertain. Autobiographies often inform and inspire.
      💌 MessageThe big lesson about life the author wants you to take away.
      🎭 ToneThe author’s attitude or feeling about the topic, like how a voice sounds when it is happy, serious, or worried.

      Tone words:

      joyfulcuriousdeterminedproudhopefulworriedgloomyhumorousseriousrespectful

      🕵️ Find tone in the author’s word choice, the details they pick, and what they emphasize.

      Your teacher can write here during the lesson. You can write too if you want!

      🧩 Sections and text features

      📋 Florida B.E.S.T. (Grade 6):
      ELA.6.R.2.1 Explain how individual text sections and/or features convey meaning in texts.

      Parts that help you understand

      • 🏷️ Headings tell what a section is mostly about.
      • 🖼️ Captions explain a picture and add details.
      • 📦 Sidebars give extra facts.
      • 🔢 Order of sections shows how ideas are organized. In an autobiography, sections often go in time order.

      Ask: “What does this part add to the whole text?”

      🌈 Figurative language

      📋 Florida B.E.S.T. (Grade 6):
      ELA.6.R.3.1 Explain how figurative language contributes to tone and meaning in text(s).

      Words that mean more than they say

      Figurative language uses words in a creative way. It adds tone and meaning.

      SimileCompares with like or as. “Fast as lightning.”
      MetaphorSays one thing is another. “The classroom was a zoo.”
      PersonificationGives human actions to non-human things. “The sun smiled down.”
      HyperboleA huge exaggeration. “I’ve told you a million times.”
      IdiomA saying that does not mean what the words say. “Break the ice.”
      AlliterationRepeated starting sounds. “Slithering snakes slid silently.”
      OnomatopoeiaA word that sounds like its meaning. “The bacon sizzled.”

      ✨ Ask three questions: What does it literally say? What does it really mean? What feeling, or tone, does it add?

      Practice passage written for this tutorial (not from the selection) When I was nine, I trailed a family of ducks around our pond every afternoon, notebook in hand. My mother worried I would fall in. My teacher said ducks were not a real subject. But I kept writing down what I saw: which duckling swam last, who quacked first, who ignored the rain. By spring, my notebook was thicker than my math book. I did not know it then, but those muddy afternoons were my first science lessons.
